## Scope and evidence status

This resource consolidates the prior ViewTube strategic responses: 10 proposed new capabilities, 20 existing-feature improvements, 30 external integrations, 15 YouTube API opportunities, repository/governance findings, priorities, architecture rules, and implementation prompts.

## Executive recommendations

1. Prioritize one connected Creator Intelligence initiative: Creator Opportunity Intelligence, Channel Intelligence, Research-to-Script Studio, Advanced YouTube Analytics, and Analytics-to-Action.
2. Treat the 75 proposals as candidates, not approved tasks. Reconcile them with the canonical idea registry, Task Authority, capability homes, active plans, and existing implementations.
3. Build provider-independent adapters; do not hardwire the product to one AI, search, transcription, stock, storage, or social provider.
4. Make evidence, source, timestamp, confidence, permission class, and freshness visible wherever recommendations or metrics are shown.
5. Preserve asset lineage from source media through edits, generated derivatives, approvals, and publication.
6. Permissions and data rights are hard eligibility gates—not merely scoring factors.
7. Avoid duplicate runtimes, registries, stores, orchestrators, and parallel capability implementations.

## Research methodology and scorecard

For each competitor and integration, capture source URL, access date, exact claim, evidence excerpt, confidence, last verified date, API/partner evidence, price basis, terms/licensing, and open questions.

Score technical/provider feasibility on nine 0–5 axes: API maturity, reliability, cost, latency, permissions, data rights, geography, substitution risk, and user value. Permissions and data rights are hard gates. Record evidence for each score; do not average away a failed hard gate.

Classify opportunities as **COPY**, **IMPROVE**, **COMBINE**, **INNOVATE**, **DEFER**, or **IGNORE**. Each proposal should include creator problem, competitor solution, competitor weakness, ViewTube improvement, dependencies, integration home, success metric, evidence, and confidence.

## Cross-cutting architecture requirements

- One canonical capability per responsibility; discover and reuse current capability homes before creating new ones.
- Provider-independent interfaces for AI, search, transcription, stock media, storage, and social publishing.
- No second Brain runtime, orchestrator, context broker, evidence store, prompt registry, or analytics store.
- Preserve asset lineage, source identity, timestamps, transformations, license/rights, and approvals.
- Keep public channel data, authorized private analytics, third-party estimates, and AI inference distinct.
- Every recommendation should explain its evidence, confidence, freshness, assumptions, effort, and expected outcome.
- Use least-privilege OAuth scopes, server-side secrets, explicit user consent, quota controls, idempotency, retries, and audit logs.
- Build deterministic validation and human approval around bulk edits, publishing, moderation, and generated media.
- Measure success through creator outcomes and workflow completion, not feature count.
